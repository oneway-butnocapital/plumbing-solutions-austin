Water Heater Repair in Austin, TX

Water heater fault checks for cold water, tank leaks, pilots, and relief valves.

Call (737) 300-9206 Ask a question

A cold shower and a puddle beside the water heater often get called the same problem. From the hallway, a dry utility room with cold taps points toward the heating side; water on the floor points to a tank, valve, or supply connection.

We handle both faults at Plumbing Solutions Austin. We inspect gas and electric units, storage tanks, shutoff valves, relief valves, and the hot-water lines leaving the heater.

No hot water can sometimes wait if the tank is dry and there is no gas smell. A leaking tank, active discharge from the relief pipe, or a gas odor should not wait because water can damage flooring and a fuel fault needs isolation.

No hot water or tank leak

People report no hot water at any faucet, a shower that goes cold halfway through, or a pilot that will not stay lit. Others find water under the tank, a steady drip from the relief-valve pipe, or popping sounds during a heating cycle.

Cold water at every fixture can trace to a failed element, thermostat, burner, gas control, or power supply. A drip may come from a relief valve, a connection, or the tank itself, so similar reports can require different parts and labor after inspection.

Tank inspection and test

We start at the tank and trace the water, gas, or electrical path. We check the shutoff, thermostat setting, pilot or ignition system, burner or elements, venting, relief valve, drain valve, and visible fittings.

We test supply voltage on electric units or gas delivery and combustion components on gas units. We also measure incoming water pressure, which is commonly maintained within 40 to 80 psi in a residential plumbing system.

A diagnostic inspection commonly takes 30 to 60 minutes. We can often replace accessible elements, thermostats, ignition parts, relief valves, or flex connections on the same visit; tank replacement, vent changes, ordered parts, and permit work are booked separately.

Tank age, shutoff, access

The tank age and failed component affect the scope of work. An accessible thermostat or relief valve is different from a corroded tank fitting, and a shutoff valve that will not close adds isolation work before a part can be changed.

A utility closet, attic platform, or tight drain-pan opening can add removal and installation work. An ordered gas control, a replacement tank, or changes needing city sign-off require separate planning and may involve another visit.

Model plate and closet access

Find the model and serial number on the rating plate, usually attached to the side of the tank. Note the approximate installation age and whether the unit uses gas, electricity, or a heat-pump system.

Tell us which room holds the tank and whether hot water is missing at every faucet or only one shower. Let us know if the tank, relief-valve discharge pipe, or floor is wet, and whether the shutoff and electrical panel are accessible.
