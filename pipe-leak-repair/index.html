Pipe Leak Repair in Austin, TX

We trace and repair leaking pipes, valves, and hidden water lines.

Call (737) 300-9206 Ask a question

A towel, bowl, or bucket under a drip is a sensible first move. It catches water and gives you time to find the shutoff valve. Tape around a small joint leak can also slow the drip for a short time.

A pipe leak usually keeps moving behind the cabinet, wall, or ceiling after the container fills. Water can follow framing, insulation, and electrical openings far from the visible stain. Pipe leak repair should not wait because a small supply-line leak can soak drywall and framing before the source is clear.

Water stains and hissing

Customers often call about a hissing sound inside a wall, a brown spot on the ceiling, or water collecting beneath a sink cabinet. A damp patch that grows after nobody has used the fixture can point to a pressurized pipe rather than a spill.

Other calls start with a water meter that keeps moving, a warm area of floor, or peeling paint near a baseboard. A wet ceiling can come from a fitting above it, while a hissing line may be hidden in a wall cavity. These signs can look alike from the room, but each leak has its own source, access work, and repair scope.

Leak inspection and pressure test

We start the callout by checking the visible pipe, shutoff valve, fixture connections, cabinet floor, and nearby wall surfaces. A visible-source inspection often takes 30 to 60 minutes. We use a moisture meter and pressure gauge to narrow down the route, with residential water pressure commonly set between 40 and 80 psi.

We can usually replace an exposed supply line, fitting, valve, or section of accessible copper, PEX, or PVC during the same visit. We isolate the water line before removing a failed connection and test the repair afterward. A leak behind tile, under a slab, or inside a finished wall may need a separate visit for access, pipe replacement, and surface restoration.

Pipe access and seized shutoffs

An exposed pipe below a cabinet takes less access work than a line behind drywall, tile, or a ceiling. A shutoff valve that will not close can expand the work because the water must be isolated elsewhere before the damaged fitting comes apart.

Pipe material also affects the repair. Aging galvanized pipe may reveal weak threads beside the first leak, while copper, PEX, and PVC use different fittings and connection methods. A special valve or fitting may need to be ordered, and work that requires inspection or sign-off can add steps beyond the leak itself.

Pipe material and access panel

Tell us which room has the leak and which fixture or appliance the pipe serves. Note whether water is running now, whether the stain is on a ceiling or wall, and whether you can reach the pipe through a cabinet, crawlspace, or access panel.

If the pipe feeds an appliance, find the model number on its manufacturer plate. A photo of the shutoff valve, fitting, and damaged area can help identify the pipe material before we arrive.
