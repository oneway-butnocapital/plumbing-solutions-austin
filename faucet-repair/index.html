Faucet Repair in Austin, TX

We repair dripping, loose, noisy, and low-flow faucets across Austin.

Call (737) 300-9206 Ask a question

People call us because “the faucet will not stop dripping,” “the handle is loose,” or “hardly any water comes out.” Those words point to parts inside the faucet body, such as a cartridge, stem, seal, aerator, or mounting nut.

We also hear about a squeal at the sink, water around the base, and a handle that turns without changing the flow. We inspect the fixture rather than assuming every drip needs the same replacement.

A slow drip may wait briefly if the faucet shuts off and the cabinet stays dry. Water running from the spout, pooling beneath the sink, or soaking the vanity should not wait because it can damage the cabinet, flooring, and supply connections.

Dripping faucet and loose handle

A faucet that drips after the handle is shut off can have a worn cartridge, damaged washer, or scored valve seat. A handle that feels loose, spins, or comes off may need a screw, adapter, stem, or handle assembly.

Low flow from one faucet often starts at a clogged aerator, especially if other fixtures have normal flow. A grinding, squealing, or clicking sound can come from the cartridge or a restricted internal passage.

Water appearing at the base of the faucet may come from worn seals, a loose mounting nut, or a connection under the sink. These symptoms can look similar from the counter, but each fault involves different parts and a different repair scope.

Sink fixture inspection

We start at the sink fixture and check the handle movement, spout flow, aerator, cartridge area, and water around the base. Under the cabinet, we inspect the shutoff valves, supply hoses, mounting hardware, and drain opening for signs that water has traveled below the countertop.

We can test water pressure at the fixture when flow points to a supply issue. Typical residential pressure is often kept between 40 and 80 psi, though a clogged aerator can reduce flow even with normal pressure in the line.

An aerator, handle screw, seal, cartridge, or supply connector can often be replaced during the same visit when the matching part is available. A specialty cartridge, discontinued finish part, seized shutoff valve, or corroded faucet mount may require a return visit after the correct component is ordered.

Cartridges, shutoffs, and access

The part inside the faucet matters. A common cartridge or aerator is usually simpler to address than a brand-specific valve assembly, a discontinued handle, or a finish-matched component that must be ordered.

Access beneath the sink also changes the work. An open cabinet with working shutoff valves allows the faucet to be isolated and removed more directly than a crowded vanity, a seized valve, or corroded hardware behind the basin.

The simpler end of the repair is an accessible fixture with an identifiable replacement part. The work becomes more involved when a shutoff will not close, the faucet is tightly corroded to the countertop, or a building manager must approve water shutdown or access.

Brand mark and room

Before you call, check which room has the faucet problem and whether it is a kitchen, bathroom, bar, laundry, or outdoor fixture. Note whether the faucet is dripping from the spout, leaking at the base, or running inside the cabinet.

Look for a brand mark on the handle, spout, or escutcheon plate. A model code may also appear in the installation booklet, receipt, product box, or on a label beneath the sink.

Tell us the approximate age of the fixture, whether either shutoff valve works, and how much room is available under the cabinet. Photos of the faucet and the connections can help identify the cartridge and mounting style.
