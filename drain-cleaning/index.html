Drain Cleaning in Austin, TX

We clear slow, blocked, gurgling, and backing-up drains across Austin.

Call (737) 300-9206 Ask a question

A sink that gurgles, a shower that holds water, or a sewer odor near a floor drain usually points to a restriction in the drain line. Grease, soap residue, hair, wipes, and roots can narrow the pipe until water no longer moves freely.

We cover drain cleaning in Austin, Rollingwood, West Lake Hills, and Lost Creek. A single slow fixture can often be scheduled, but wastewater backing up, an overflowing toilet, or several drains stopping together should not wait because the blockage may be in a shared branch or sewer line.

Gurgling drains and sewer odor

Customers often report a kitchen sink that drains slowly, a bathtub that fills around their feet, or a washing machine drain that overflows. Other common signs include bubbles in a toilet bowl after the shower runs and a sewage smell coming from a floor drain.

A clog close to the fixture can act much like a blockage farther down the branch line. A dry trap can also create an odor without a solid blockage. We inspect the pipe and fixture because these symptoms can look alike from the room, yet each fault needs different work and can involve different materials and access.

Drain inspection and cable callout

We start the callout by checking which fixture is affected and whether nearby drains change when water runs. We test the drain flow, inspect the trap where accessible, and check cleanout openings before selecting a cable or other cleaning method.

Most household drain branches range from 1 1/2 to 4 inches in diameter. A fixture-level check and clearing can often be completed during the same visit. We may recommend a camera inspection or schedule further work if the blockage sits beyond the available cleanout, the line has root intrusion, or the pipe needs repair.

Pipe access and blockage depth

The route to the blockage affects the work. A cleanout near the exterior wall gives us a different path than a line reached through a cabinet, crawlspace, or roof vent. A clog near the trap generally takes less setup than debris farther along a branch or sewer line.

Pipe material and condition also matter. Older cast iron can hold scale inside the pipe, while a damaged clay or plastic line may need a camera inspection after cleaning. Roots, a seized cleanout cap, restricted access, and a line that requires property or municipal sign-off can add steps before the drain is fully cleared.

Fixture location and cleanout access

Tell us which room has the slow or backing-up fixture, such as the kitchen sink, shower, laundry standpipe, or floor drain. Let us know whether one drain is affected or whether water appears in a different fixture when another one runs.

If you can see a cleanout cap, tell us where it is located: outside, in a utility room, under a sink, or in a crawlspace. Keep cabinets, floor drains, and access panels clear where possible. Mention any recent grease, wipes, food scraps, or construction debris that may have entered the line.
